Estimation Approach
Our preferred estimator follows Sun and Abraham (2021), which addresses the negative
weighting problem that can arise in standard two-way fixed effects (TWFE) models under
staggered treatment timing (Goodman-Bacon,2021). For robustness, we also estimate the
model using the TWFE specification and the approach ofCallaway and Sant’Anna(2021).
The estimated effects are qualitatively similar across methods. This consistency is expected
given the presence of a large never-treated group, such that most of the identifying variation
comes from comparisons between treated and untreated tracts.
Identification relies on two key assumptions. First, we assume parallel trends: in the
absence of data center entry, inefficient pricing in treated and untreated tracts would have
evolved along similar trajectories. We provide evidence on this assumption through pre-
treatment event-study coefficients in Section6.1. Second, we assume no anticipation: out-
comes do not respond to data center entry before the facility becomes operational. Although
data centers involve long planning and permitting timelines, grid-level impacts- particularly
congestion and marginal loss effects, are driven by actual electricity consumption rather than
the announcement or construction of a facility. We therefore define treatment as the year a
data center begins drawing power from the grid.
