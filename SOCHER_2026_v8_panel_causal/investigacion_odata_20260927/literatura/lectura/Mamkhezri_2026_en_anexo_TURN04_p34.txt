Using the estimated propensity scores, we implementk−nearest neighbor (kNN) match-
ing with replacement, matching each treated tract to its ten nearest untreated neighbors
(10NN). This process pairs treated tracts with similar untreated tracts based on observed
pre-treatment characteristics, restricting the sample to comparable units.

5.2 Second Stage: Staggered Difference-in-Differences Models
In the second stage, we estimate the impact of data center entry on transmission-related
price distortions using a staggered difference-in-differences design. The specification is:
Yit =α+βDCit +γi +ωt +ϵit, (3)
where Yit denotes inefficient pricing, defined as the combination of congestion and marginal
loss components of LMP in census tracti and yeart. The indicatorDCit equals one for tract
i in all yearst at or after its first data center entry and zero otherwise. The model includes
tractfixedeffects γi andyearfixedeffects ωt. Becausepropensityscorematchingisconducted
with replacement, some untreated census tracts may appear multiple times in the matched
sample. To account for this, we weight each observation by the inverse of the number
of times a tract appears in the matched dataset. Identification relies on variation in the
congestion and marginal loss components, which reflect spatial price distortions associated
with transmission constraints. Standard errors are clustered at the census tract level.
