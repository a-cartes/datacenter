Spillovers of Inefficient Pricing
Another concern is that congestion induced by data center entry may spill over to nearby
tracts. In a meshed transmission network, congestion at one location can alter power flows
and prices at distant nodes through loop flows, potentially violating SUTVA. If untreated
tracts are affected by congestion originating in treated tracts, the control group outcomes
are contaminated, biasing the estimated effect toward zero (if spillovers raise control group
prices) or away from zero (if spillovers lower control group prices through rerouting).
To examine this possibility, we compute pairwise correlations in pre-treatment inefficient
pricing across all census tracts in Virginia. We identify untreated tracts that are highly cor-
related with treated tracts and exclude untreated tracts with correlation coefficients greater
than 0.5 relative to treated tracts. This procedure restricts the comparison group to tracts
that are less likely to be affected by spillovers through the transmission network.
The restricted sample includes 20 treated tracts and 30 untreated tracts. Appendix Table
A.7 Panel A reports estimates using this lower-correlation untreated group. The results are
consistent with the baseline aggregate estimates, suggesting that spillovers are unlikely to
drive the main findings.15
6.4.2 Matching
Our results are robust to alternative matching procedures. Appendix Table A.7 reports
estimates from several alternative specifications: models without propensity score matching
(Panel B), models not weighted by tract duplication arising from matching with replacement
(Panel C), models excluding duplicated tracts entirely (Panel D), and models using five
nearest neighbors (5NN) instead of ten (10NN) for matching (Panel E). The estimated
effects remain stable across specifications, indicating that the findings are not sensitive to
the matching design.
